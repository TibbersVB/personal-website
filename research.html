Research

My research lies at the intersection of health economics, intellectual property and market access. I study how complex patent landscapes shape competition, pricing, R&D incentives and, ultimately, access to medicines and vaccines.

PhD Thesis Oct. 2025 – ongoing

Fragmentation of intellectual property in the biopharmaceutical ecosystem: implications for innovation and access to medicines and vaccines

PhD in Economics · University of Lille · LEM (UMR 9221, CNRS)

Supervisors: Etienne Billette de Villemeur, Bruno Versaevel

My thesis examines how fragmented intellectual property structures in the biopharmaceutical sector affect innovation incentives and access to medicines and vaccines. It explores patent networks, their dynamics, and their impact on market access and R&D investment, applying advanced econometric methods and pharmacoeconomic models to analyze the regulatory and policy impacts on pharmaceutical markets.
